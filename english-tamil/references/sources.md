# Linguistic evidence and scope

The references support broad linguistic distinctions. They do not certify every
example, determine a universal switch frequency or validate an arbitrary generated
corpus. The mixed-script spelling, tag inventory, hyphens and checker thresholds
are annotation/tooling choices. Named varieties and study populations matter.

## Tamil morphology and English material

Sankoff, Poplack and Vanniarajan, *The case of the nonce loan in Tamil* (1990),
analyze English-origin material integrated into Tamil grammatical frames,
including case marking. The publisher abstract distinguishes integrated
borrowings from their theoretical account of code-switching. It supports taking
morphological integration seriously; it does not justify rejecting every
single-word insertion or equating script boundaries with linguistic switches.
Only the abstract is relied on here.
[Publisher abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-variation-and-change/article/abs/case-of-the-nonce-loan-in-tamil/F7F49FF1CB164298C640EC5EF0573C7E).

Sanmuganathan, *Grammatical Constraints in Tamil-English Code Mixing among the
Urban Jaffna Tamils* (2020), illustrates English material with Tamil grammatical
support, including light verbs and suffixes. Its urban Jaffna speaker sample is
not a Singapore sample; its examples and categorical claims should not become
universal restrictions on all Tamil–English speakers.
[Original paper](https://languageinindia.com/march2020/drsanmuganathancodemixingjaffnatamilenglish2.pdf).

## Register

Prasanna and Arora, *IruMozhi: Automatically classifying diglossia in Tamil*
(2024), distinguish literary and spoken varieties in a computational resource.
This supports treating register separately from script. It does not validate
this rulebook's outputs or establish one Singapore spoken norm.
[Research paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.195/).

The [spoken-Tamil reference](spoken_tamil.md) includes further grammar and
Singapore classroom sources, with their community and task limits. Full forms
can occur naturally in speech; colloquial rendering is not global word replacement.

## Singapore particles

Particle meanings depend on discourse and intonation. Lee's *An Analysis of
Colloquial Singapore English lah and Its Interpretation across Speech Acts*
(2022) analyzes particular uses and contours; the rulebook's short glosses should
be used as review prompts rather than exhaustive definitions.
[Original article](https://doi.org/10.3390/languages7030203).

Leimgruber, Lim, Gonzales and Hiramoto, *Ethnic and gender variation in the use of
Colloquial Singapore English discourse particles* (2021), study a text-message
corpus and social variation. Such evidence does not set spoken Tamil–English
particle quotas or make any particle obligatory.
[Publisher article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/english-language-and-linguistics/article/ethnic-and-gender-variation-in-the-use-of-colloquial-singapore-english-discourse-particles/C988725C830A55A0B022BB4118689236).

## Using the evidence

Treat the worked pairs as authored illustrations with stated contexts, not as
quotations from these papers or a human-certified gold set. Community review can
resolve uncertain local usage. For audio, inspect pronunciation and discourse
contour as well as the written text. Neither a citation nor a machine gate proves
that a particular synthetic utterance sounds natural or improves recognition.
