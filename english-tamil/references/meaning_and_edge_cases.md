# Meaning checks and difficult inputs

Use this alongside the grammar reference. These are decision rules for rewriting
and review, not additional facts to supply to an underspecified source.

## Quantities, units, times and scope

| Source distinction | Failure to catch | What to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| The bill increased **by** $50. | The bill became $50. | Size of the increase; the final total is not supplied. |
| The bill increased **to** $50. | It increased by $50. | Final total; the size of the increase is not supplied. |
| **At least** three people came. | Exactly three came. | Lower bound and uncertainty above it. |
| **Not all** files uploaded. | No files uploaded. | Partial failure does not establish total failure. |
| **Only Meena** sent the file. | Meena sent only the file. | Who is restricted versus what is restricted. |
| Finish **before** lunch. | Finish after lunch, or by an invented clock time. | Ordering without inventing a time. |
| Wait **until** Monday. | Finish by Monday. | Continuing an activity versus a completion deadline. |
| Come **after** work. | Come after an invented meeting. | The specified reference event. |
| **If** the bus is late, call me. | The bus is late, so call me. | Condition versus asserted event and cause. |

Do not replace “before today” with “by today” merely because one sounds more
usual. Preserve the relation even if the unusual source needs clarification.
Do not double-mark an English relation with an incompatible Tamil relation.
`lunch-க்கு முன்னாடி` can express “before lunch”; its presence does not authorize
changing a source that instead says “after lunch.”

Preserve currency, measurements, addresses, identifiers and decimal values.
Spelling out a number is an annotation/TTS choice only when the reading is clear;
`03/04` should not acquire an invented date interpretation. Keep an ambiguous
acronym or identifier intact and ask for its pronunciation policy when needed.

## Negation, ability and obligation

Compare the proposition, not just whether a negative-looking token exists:

- “Did not upload” describes non-occurrence.
- “Could not upload” describes inability in the relevant context.
- “Must not upload” prohibits an action.
- “Need not upload” removes an obligation; it does not prohibit uploading.
- “Will not upload” may express a future negative or refusal, depending on context.
- “Has not uploaded yet” carries an aspectual/time relation that a bare negative
  can lose.

Possible Tamil contrasts include `upload பண்ணல` for a contextual negative action
and `upload பண்ண முடியல` for inability. These are not interchangeable. Choose
person, time and register from the full source rather than replacing every
English negative with one familiar Tamil ending.

## Participants, kinship and respectful language

For “Meena's older brother,” preserve both possession and the specified relation:
`Meena-ஓட அண்ணா` can make the possessive relation explicit. A bare name followed
by a kin term can instead be read as address or apposition.

For “Meena's relative,” do not invent “older brother” or “mother.” English
“sibling,” “cousin,” “aunt” and “uncle” do not always specify the distinctions
required by a more specific Tamil kinship term. Retain a suitably broad expression
or request clarification if a narrower term would add facts.

Names do not establish gender, ethnicity or family role. A respectful pronoun
may refer to one person; do not turn respectful singular into factual plural.
English “you” leaves number and social relationship open in many contexts.
Use the declared register and context, or retain ambiguity; do not add intimate
address terms, scolding or a new social relationship to create a switch.

## Questions, quotations and reported speech

Keep a question's intent: information seeking, yes/no confirmation, permission
and a rhetorical challenge can differ. A final question mark alone does not
prove that the same question survived. “Where is the file?” must not become
“Is the file here?” or acquire a fabricated answer.

For reported speech, distinguish the narrator, reporting speaker and quoted
speaker. “Mother said, ‘Call me later’” is not a direct request from the narrator.
Preserve required verbatim quotes; if translation of quoted content is permitted,
retain the original meaning and label the transformation appropriately.

“Meena said Arun could call her” may contain an ambiguous referent. Do not resolve
“her” to a named person without context. Check whether a pronoun shift after a
switch changes the viewpoint or addressee.

## Multiple switches and long turns

For each clause, write a short review trace of participant, event, time/polarity
and connection to adjacent clauses. Then check the complete turn. For example:

> I sent the file yesterday, but there is still no reply. Can you check it?

The review must retain past sending, yesterday, absent reply up to now, the
contrast, and the request to check. A result that says the file was not sent,
that a reply arrived, or that the recipient has already checked it is wrong even
if all switch boundaries look fluent.

Natural word order may differ between source and target. Do not require a
switch at the same token index as another candidate; do require that each
clause remain grammatically supported and that the turn retain its endpoint.
Do not join two unfinished clauses by adding a causative connective. More
switches do not compensate for missing content or changed relationships.

## Fragments, hesitation and repetition

A short source such as “Not now” may remain a short reply. Do not infer what
activity is postponed. “Yes” may not support natural two-language wording without
new content: retain it when monolingual output is allowed, or flag a strict-mixed
requirement conflict. Do not add a name or filler to satisfy a script detector.

Distinguish a meaningful correction (“Tuesday—sorry, Wednesday”) from accidental
duplication. In faithful transcription retain the audible repair under the
chosen policy. In rewriting preserve its communicative function unless cleanup
is authorized. Do not automatically strip repetitions, complete abandoned
sentences or add an invented continuation to a finished short utterance.

## Loanwords, names and script uncertainty

A Tamil-script word may be an established borrowing, an English item rendered
phonetically, or a different Tamil word with a similar spelling. Contextual
language annotation cannot be replaced by an etymology lookup. Likewise, a Latin
name or number is not sufficient evidence of an English phrase.

Resolve the source meaning and chosen orthography first. The mapping file is a
candidate list, and the spelling policy is a local convention. If the task is
faithful transcription, listen or retain the uncertainty instead of translating
to make an automated language counter look balanced.

## When to defer

Use a pending decision when material facts, language identity or required local
usage remain uncertain. Record the exact issue and the smallest clarification
needed. A coherent invented answer is not an acceptable repair for missing source
meaning. An agent can continue other rows while the affected row stays unresolved.
