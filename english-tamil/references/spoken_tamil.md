# Spoken Tamil, written Tamil and register

## Choose register independently of script

For conversational Singapore English–Tamil generation, prefer contextually
natural spoken Tamil. Tamil Unicode can write both spoken and literary Tamil;
using Tamil characters does not require literary vocabulary or verb forms.
Keep English anchors in Latin and Tamil case, agreement, tense, negation and
quotative grammar in Tamil. Neither English-heavy wording nor romanization
determines whether the Tamil itself is conversational.

Preserve formal register when required by a source, quotation, reading or brief.
For faithful ASR transcription, preserve what was actually spoken, including
formal language. A public-service topic alone does not require literary Tamil:
an informal explanation and a formal announcement can discuss the same service.

## Contextual pairs, not replacements

These are author-written teaching examples, not corpus quotations or experimentally
validated translations. The left column uses fuller/written-style forms; the
right gives a possible conversational realization in the stated context. Some
full forms also occur naturally in speech. Judge the complete utterance.

| Fuller form | Possible spoken form | Meaning and context to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `அது ready-ஆ இருக்கிறது.` | `அது ready-ஆ இருக்கு.` | “It is ready”: a current singular state. Do not turn past readiness into present readiness. |
| `இங்கே இடம் இல்லை.` | `இங்க இடம் இல்ல.` | “There is no space here”: current negative existence. `இல்லை` can also be natural in emphatic correction or more formal speech. |
| `நேற்று அவர் வரவில்லை.` | `நேத்து அவர் வரல.` | “They did not come yesterday”: one respectfully referred-to person; yesterday anchors the past. This does not mean they could not come or will not come. |
| `எனக்குத் தெரியவில்லை.` | `எனக்குத் தெரியல.` | “I do not know” in a current-knowledge context. A past narrative needs its own temporal interpretation. |
| `இப்போது என்னால் upload செய்ய முடியவில்லை.` | `இப்போ என்னால upload பண்ண முடியல.` | “I cannot upload now”: retain inability; `upload பண்ணல` alone does not preserve that modal meaning. |
| `நீங்கள் file-ஐ அனுப்புங்கள்.` | `நீங்க file-ஐ அனுப்புங்க.` | “Please send the file”: respectful singular or plural address, as established by context. Do not reduce this to familiar singular `நீ … அனுப்பு`. |
| `அம்மா "call me later" என்று சொன்னார்.` | `அம்மா "call me later"-னு சொன்னாங்க.` | “Mother said, ‘call me later’”: preserve the speaker, respect, past reporting and quoted words. |

Do not map every form of a verb to its shortest form. `இருந்தேன்`, `இருப்பேன்`
and `இருக்கும்` differ from `இருக்கு` in person or temporal/modal interpretation;
`கேக்க வேண்டியிருக்கும்` must retain its prospective obligation. Likewise,
negated action, inability, prohibition and refusal are different meanings.
Preserve polarity and the question type when changing an ending or word order.

Colloquial language retains respect and agreement. Do not infer a person's
gender from a name, replace a respectful pronoun with a familiar one, or turn a
quoted instruction into the narrator's instruction. Preserve required verbatim
quoted words; a spoken reporting frame does not license rewriting the quote.

## Variation and review

Forms such as `எங்கே` and `எங்க` need not receive the same judgment in every
context. Speaker community, relationship, emphasis and discourse affect choices;
do not treat one Tamil Nadu variety or one spelling as the Singapore norm.
Do not impose a minimum reduced-form count, mandatory slang or a literary-word
blacklist. Sparse marker scans can locate candidates, but cannot decide register
or semantic equivalence.

During the existing separate whole-row review, inspect source intent, the declared
register and the full candidate together. Record a contextual repair or explain
why a full form is appropriate. Edited text requires fresh review. Escalate
unresolved local usage to Singapore Tamil native speakers familiar with the
target setting, with audio/intonation where relevant. Model-assisted judgments
must not be described as native-speaker certification.

## Primary evidence and its limits

- Harold F. Schiffman's *A Reference Grammar of Spoken Tamil* describes a spoken
  variety distinct from literary Tamil. It supports the register distinction,
  not a claim that every Singapore speaker uses one standard form.
  [University of Pennsylvania book page](https://www.southasia.upenn.edu/books/reference-grammar-spoken-tamil).
- Seetha Lakshmi (2012), *The Impact of Standard Spoken Tamil in Singapore Tamil
  Classrooms*, draws on 18 primary Tamil lessons and teacher interviews. It
  distinguishes written Tamil from writing spoken Tamil and discusses spoken
  variation and formal use. Its classroom evidence is not an ASR benchmark or
  validation of this table. [Original publisher PDF](https://www.ncolctl.org/files/the-impact.pdf).
- Kabilan Prasanna and Aryaman Arora (2024), *IruMozhi: Automatically classifying
  diglossia in Tamil*, pairs 499 literary sentences with spoken translations.
  The two translators grew up in Salem, Tamil Nadu; this is evidence for a
  computationally relevant register distinction, not a Singapore usage standard
  or a classifier adopted by this project.
  [NAACL Findings paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.195/).
